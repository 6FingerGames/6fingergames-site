DIVER: player's guide

One breath, rising multipliers and how to come back with the treasure

DIVER is a one-button game with a very simple rule: your score only counts if you come back to the surface alive. Everything else, the sharks, the jellyfish and the dark, is there to make that harder. Here is how the dive really works. You can play the game right here: Play DIVER.

The rules of a dive

You get one breath of air. Hold a finger or the mouse button to sink, and let go to float up. You can also steer left and right to dodge what swims at you. Coins and treasure add to your score, but until you reach the surface they are only a promise: if the air runs out first, the haul is lost.

Controls

  • Dive: hold the mouse button, a finger on the screen, or the keyboard.
  • Rise: let go. You float up on your own.
  • Steer: move the mouse, drag your finger, or use the keyboard to guide the diver sideways.
  • Language: English, Georgian, Russian and Chinese are available from the menu.

Reading the ocean

  • Jellyfish drift slowly. They are easy to avoid, but they sit exactly where treasure likes to be.
  • Sharks cruise through the water and are far more dangerous than they look. Do not try to squeeze past one.
  • Anglerfish glow in the dark, so you can see them coming. A light in the gloom is a warning, not a reward.

Hits cost you air as well as safety, so a hurt diver has to turn back sooner.

The multiplier gamble

The deeper you swim, the higher your score multiplier climbs, and the angrier the ocean gets. This is the main decision of the game. Deep treasure is worth much more, but you must still make the trip back up, and your air drains the same way at every depth.

A good rule is to turn around when you have about half of your air left. That leaves enough to rise, dodge something on the way and still reach the surface.

Air management

Air bubbles and oxygen tanks stretch the dive. They are worth a small detour, but not a dangerous one: a tank next to a shark is usually a trap. At the surface your air refills, so short, safe dives that bank points often can beat one greedy plunge.

Quick tips

  • Bank early: surface to save points before you go deeper.
  • Turn back at half air, not at one quarter.
  • Let hazards pass before you commit to a gap.
  • Treasure near hazards is rarely worth it in the first few dives.
