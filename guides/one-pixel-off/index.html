ONE PIXEL OFF: player's guide

Timing, perfect streaks, the scoring and how to win width back

ONE PIXEL OFF gives you one button and a tower of candy blocks. A new block slides above the tower, you tap, and it drops. Whatever hangs over the edge is sliced off for good, so every careless tap makes the rest of the climb harder. This guide explains what the game measures, how the points really add up and why patience beats speed. You can play the game right here: Play ONE PIXEL OFF.

Controls

  • Drop the block: tap the screen, click, or press Space, Enter or the Down arrow.
  • Pause: P or Escape. Switching to another tab pauses the game by itself.
  • Sound on or off: M, or the speaker button.
  • Language: English, Georgian, Russian, Chinese and Japanese, in Settings.

For a split second after a new block appears, taps are ignored. That is on purpose: a double tap or a touch screen that registers one touch twice should never cost you a block.

How the blocks move

Each block comes in from the far side of the tower and slides across it along one of the two diagonals. The next block uses the other diagonal, so the direction keeps switching as you climb. A block that reaches the end of its path turns around and comes back.

There is no timer. A block will swing back and forth for as long as you let it, so if a pass feels wrong, simply wait for the next one. Most early runs end because of a rushed tap, not a hard block.

Slices and misses

When you drop a block off-center, only the part that sits on the block below stays. The rest is cut off and falls away, and the next block arrives at the new, narrower size. If the block misses the tower entirely, the run is over.

Two things on screen help you aim. The dashed white outline marks the edges of the block below, which is your real target. The soft dark patch under the moving block shows how much of it would stay if you tapped right now. Watch the patch fill the outline, and tap when it does.

Perfect landings

Drop a block close enough to dead center and it snaps exactly onto the block below: nothing is cut, a ring of light flashes and "PERFECT!" pops up. Despite the name, the game is a little kinder than one pixel. The allowed gap is small, but it does not shrink when your blocks get narrow, so a thin tower is just as easy to land perfectly as a wide one.

Speed is what makes it hard. The blocks get faster with every level until roughly the sixtieth block, where they stop speeding up. At the start the perfect window lasts about a tenth of a second as the block passes; at top speed it is close to half of that.

Streaks and growing back

Perfect landings in a row build a streak, shown next to your score. From the third perfect in a row, every further perfect also gives the block some of its lost width back, until it reaches its original size again. A clumsy stretch in the middle of a run is not the end: three calm taps in a row start repairing the tower.

At a streak of five, the edges of the screen start to glow gold and sparkles trail the moving block. Any drop that is not perfect resets the streak to zero.

How scoring works

An ordinary landing is worth 1 point. A perfect landing is worth 2 points plus your current streak, so the first perfect gives 3, the second 4, the third 5 and so on. Ten perfects in a row earn 75 points, while ten ordinary drops earn 10. That is why the best scores come from long streaks, not from tall towers alone.

Every ten blocks the game cheers you on: Sweet! at 10, Yummy! at 20, Tasty! at 30, Delicious! at 40 and Legendary! from 50 up. At the end of a run you see your score, your best score, how many blocks you placed and how many of them were perfect.

Continue

If your run ends after at least four blocks, a Continue button may appear. It plays an optional ad, and only after it finishes do you carry on from the same tower, with your streak starting again from zero. It is offered once per run and only when an ad is actually available; if there is none, the game tells you so instead of pretending.

Tips from our own runs

  • Never chase a block. Let it pass and take the next swing.
  • Look at the dashed outline, not at the moving block's edges.
  • When the tower is already thin, play only for perfects: an off-center drop costs a narrow block much more than a wide one.
  • After two perfects in a row, slow down. The third is the one that starts giving width back.
  • Your best score is saved the moment you beat it, so closing the tab mid-run does not lose it.
