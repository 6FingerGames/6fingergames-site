SWARM: player's guide

Gravity, combos, anomalies and how to keep the clock alive

SWARM is simple to start and surprisingly easy to lose at. You glide a gravity well across the sky, collect glowing lights into an orbiting swarm, and use its pull to drag spaceships into a hungry black hole. This guide explains how the pieces fit together so your first runs last longer. You can play the game right here: Play SWARM.

The basics

Move the mouse (or drag one finger) and your swarm follows. Every glowing light you touch joins the orbit around you. The bigger the swarm, the stronger its gravity. Ships are heavy, so a tiny swarm will barely nudge them, while a big one can drag them across the screen and into the black hole.

Each ship you feed to the hole gives you combo points and a few extra seconds on the clock. The clock never stops, so the game is a constant trade between gathering more lights and cashing in the ones you already have.

Controls

  • Mouse or touch: move to steer the swarm. On a phone, one finger is all you need.
  • Pulse: press Space on a keyboard, or the pulse button on a touch screen. It shoves nearby ships away for a moment and then needs a few seconds to recharge. Use it when something dangerous gets too close, not as a habit.
  • Language: choose English, Georgian, Russian or Chinese from the menu.

Growing a strong swarm

Early on, do not chase ships. Spend the first seconds of a level collecting lights in wide loops, because every light you add makes the later pulls easier. A swarm that has only a handful of lights will lose a tug of war with the first ship it meets.

Keep your movement smooth. Sudden jerks fling lights out of orbit, while a steady circle keeps them tight around you.

Dragging ships into the hole

Approach a ship from the side opposite the black hole, let the swarm take hold, then move toward the hole slowly. The ship follows the gravity of your swarm, so you lead it like a dog on a lead. Pulling too fast makes it slip away.

Feeding several ships in a row builds a combo, and a combo multiplies the points of each ship. If the clock is short, a quick feed is often better than a perfect one, because every ship also adds time.

The purple anomalies

The purple anomalies are the danger of the game. If one touches your swarm, it wrecks it. You cannot fight them, so learn the rhythm instead: gather, pull, step away. When an anomaly drifts toward you, lead your lights around it rather than trying to outrun it in a straight line.

You have a small number of lives, shown as hearts. Losing a swarm costs one, so a careful run beats a greedy one.

Quick tips

  • Gather first, hunt later.
  • Move in smooth arcs, not zigzags.
  • Keep the pulse for emergencies.
  • Watch the clock: a quick ship is worth more than a perfect one.
  • Your best score is saved in your own browser, so keep playing on the same device.
